Scam Awareness

Recognize the warning signs before you respond

Learn how common scams work, what suspicious messages may look like, and how to take safer next steps when something does not feel right.

Important: The cards below describe common scam patterns for awareness and education. They are not live incident reports, verified breaking news, or claims that a specific person or organization is currently involved in fraud. Always verify current warnings through official sources.

Warning signs to look out for

A single warning sign does not always prove that something is a scam. Be especially cautious when several signs appear together.

1

Unexpected urgency

Someone pressures you to pay, click a link, reveal a code, or make a decision immediately.

2

Unusual payment requests

You are asked to pay through cryptocurrency, gift cards, unfamiliar accounts, or another hard-to-reverse method.

3

Requests for secrets

A caller or message asks for your password, PIN, seed phrase, or one-time authentication code.

Recovery scams

Fake fund recovery offers

Someone claims they can retrieve money lost to fraud, often after finding your public post or contacting you unexpectedly.

  • They promise guaranteed results.
  • They demand advance fees or repeated payments.
  • They claim special access to frozen or missing funds.

Safer response: Contact your bank or payment provider directly and verify any service independently.

Financial scams

Investment and trading promises

A person or platform promotes unusually high returns with little or no risk, or pressures you to invest before you can verify the details.

  • Returns are described as guaranteed.
  • You are urged to recruit others or act immediately.
  • You must pay additional fees to withdraw supposed profits.

Safer response: Verify the company and relevant financial authorization through official registers. Do not rely on screenshots of profits.

Identity and impersonation

Bank or government impersonation

Someone pretends to represent a bank, public agency, police service, or another trusted organization to make you panic or comply.

  • They threaten arrest, account closure, or penalties unless you pay.
  • They request a password or verification code.
  • They tell you to transfer money to a supposedly safe account.

Safer response: End the unexpected contact and reach the organization using independently verified details.

Relationship scams

Romance and friendship scams

Someone builds trust online and later asks for money, financial help, investment deposits, or sensitive personal information.

  • The relationship develops unusually quickly.
  • They repeatedly avoid independent identity checks or live conversations.
  • They introduce emergencies, travel costs, or investment opportunities.

Safer response: Do not send money or financial details to someone you have not independently verified. Talk to a trusted person before making a decision.

Online and shopping scams

Fake shops and marketplace listings

A seller advertises a product at an unusually low price but may send nothing, provide a counterfeit item, or move the transaction off-platform.

  • Prices are far below typical market rates without a credible explanation.
  • The seller pressures you to pay outside the platform.
  • Reviews, photos, or business details appear copied or inconsistent.

Safer response: Check seller history, use protected payment methods, and keep transaction records.

Account and device security

Phishing links and fake login pages

A message leads you to a page designed to steal login credentials, payment details, or authentication codes.

  • The message creates urgency or fear.
  • The web address imitates a trusted service.
  • The page asks you to sign in after an unexpected message.

Safer response: Open the service using its official app or a trusted bookmark. If you entered a password, change it promptly from a trusted device.

Financial scams

Unexpected payment or delivery messages

A message claims you owe a small fee, need to confirm a delivery, or must fix a payment problem through a link.

  • The message was not expected.
  • The link leads to an unfamiliar or misspelled domain.
  • You are asked for card details or a one-time code to resolve the issue.

Safer response: Check delivery or payment status directly through the provider's official app or website.

Identity and impersonation

Job offers and recruitment fraud

A supposed employer offers a job but asks for upfront payments, sensitive documents too early, or money transfers as part of the role.

  • You must pay to receive a job or guaranteed placement.
  • You are asked to deposit a cheque and send some money elsewhere.
  • The employer's identity and contact details cannot be verified.

Safer response: Verify the employer independently and do not forward money on behalf of a stranger.

Online and shopping scams

Giveaway and prize scams

A message claims you have won a prize, but you must pay a fee or provide sensitive details before receiving it.

  • You did not enter the competition.
  • You are asked to pay a release or processing fee to an unknown person.
  • The organizer cannot be verified through an official source.

Safer response: Verify the organizer and rules independently. Do not pay unexpected fees or share authentication codes.

Account and device security

Remote-access and tech support scams

Someone claims your device has a serious problem and asks you to install software or grant remote access so they can supposedly fix it.

  • You receive an unexpected warning or support call.
  • You are instructed to install unfamiliar remote-control software.
  • The caller asks you to sign in to financial accounts while they watch.

Safer response: Do not grant unsolicited access. If you already did, disconnect the session, secure exposed accounts from another trusted device, and seek technical help.

What to do when you spot a scam

1

Pause and verify

Do not rush into a payment or reveal information. Verify the sender, company, and request using a trusted independent channel.

2

Protect your accounts

If you shared credentials or payment details, secure the affected accounts and contact your bank or service provider promptly.

3

Keep evidence and report

Save relevant messages, transaction references, screenshots, and dates. Report through the appropriate platform, provider, or authority.

Already paid or shared information?

You can still take useful steps. Contact your bank, card issuer, mobile-money provider, or payment service as soon as possible. Ask what protective actions are available for your transaction. Change exposed passwords, secure linked accounts, and preserve evidence.

Avoid anyone who guarantees a refund or asks for another payment to release recovered funds. Recovery is not guaranteed, and procedures vary by provider, payment method, and jurisdiction.

Frequently asked questions

Are these alerts reports of scams happening right now?

No. The cards on this page explain common scam patterns for educational purposes. They do not confirm a current incident, identify a current perpetrator, or replace official warnings.

How can I check whether a warning is genuine?

Look for the warning on the relevant organization's official website. Verify the web address independently and compare the information with other reliable sources. Be cautious of screenshots or forwarded messages that provide no verifiable source.

Should I forward suspicious messages to friends?

Avoid spreading unverified claims or suspicious links. If you want to warn others, describe the general warning signs and link to a reliable official source. Use the platform's reporting tools where available.

What if I cannot tell whether a message is a scam?

Do not use the links or contact details in the message to verify it. Open the official service independently, contact the organization using trusted details, or ask a trusted person to review the request before you act.

Can Scam Recovery Help investigate a scam or recover funds?

No. This website is an independent educational resource. It does not investigate individual incidents, trace payments, access accounts, or guarantee financial recovery. Contact the appropriate provider and authorities for help with a specific incident.
