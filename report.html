Reporting guidance

Report the scam. Protect your next step.

Reporting can help alert financial providers and authorities, preserve a record of what happened, and potentially prevent further harm. Choose your country below to find relevant official resources.

Important: Scam Recovery Help is an independent educational website, not a government agency or police service. We do not receive or investigate reports, contact banks on your behalf, or recover funds. Use the official external services listed below to make a report.

What to do before you report

If money or personal information has been taken, take these steps as soon as practical. Do not delay contacting your bank while trying to complete every item on this page.

1

Contact your bank or payment provider

Use the official app, website, or number on your card. Explain the suspected fraud and ask whether a payment can be stopped, recalled, disputed, or investigated.

2

Secure your accounts

Change compromised passwords from a trusted device. Secure your email, enable multi-factor authentication, and ask your provider to block unauthorized access where possible.

3

Preserve your evidence

Keep transaction receipts, dates, messages, email headers, usernames, website addresses, phone numbers, and case references. Do not alter original files if you can avoid it.

4

Make an official report

Choose the appropriate reporting route for your country and the type of fraud. Follow the official website's instructions and keep your report reference.

5

Report the account or platform

If the scam happened through a social network, marketplace, messaging service, or website, report the account or listing through that platform's official tools.

6

Watch for follow-up scams

Scammers may contact victims again pretending to be investigators or recovery agents. Never share passwords, one-time codes, or crypto wallet recovery phrases.

Common reporting questions

Should I contact my bank before filing a police report?

If money or payment credentials are involved, contact your bank or payment provider immediately. You can contact them while preparing a police or government report; do not wait for one process to finish before notifying the other.

Does filing a report mean I will get my money back?

No. Reporting creates a record and may help authorities or providers assess the incident, but it does not guarantee an investigation, a chargeback, a reversal, or recovery of funds.

Should I pay someone who says they can recover my money?

Be very cautious. A person who guarantees recovery, demands an upfront fee, requests your password or wallet recovery phrase, or claims they need a payment to release funds may be running another scam.

What if I only received a suspicious message?

You may still be able to report the message to the relevant national scam-reporting service and the platform where it appeared. Do not click links or open unexpected attachments just to investigate.

Can I report a scam that happened in another country?

You can usually notify your local police or national reporting service and the financial provider involved. The best route depends on where you live, where the incident occurred, and the payment method. Use official channels for guidance.

Keep your evidence and protect your accounts

After reporting, save the reference number and continue securing your accounts. Recovery outcomes vary, and no website or agent can honestly guarantee that your funds will be returned.
