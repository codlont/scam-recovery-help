Recovery Guide

Take the next step after a scam.

If you have lost money, shared personal information, or given someone access to an account, prompt action can help protect what remains and preserve any available options. Start with the steps that match your situation.

Start with immediate steps Organize your evidence

First priority: limit further loss

Contact your bank, card issuer, mobile-money provider, cryptocurrency platform, or other payment service through independently verified official channels. Explain that you suspect fraud and ask what urgent actions are available. If your account or device is compromised, secure it from a trusted device. Do not wait to finish this guide before reporting an urgent incident.

What to do first

These steps are a general guide. The right process depends on the payment method, provider, country, and how long ago the incident occurred.

1

Contact the payment provider

Use the official number on your bank card, the provider's verified website, or the secure app you normally use.

  • State clearly that you suspect a scam or unauthorized transaction.
  • Ask whether a transfer can be stopped, recalled, traced, or disputed.
  • Ask about card cancellation, account restrictions, and replacement credentials where appropriate.
  • Request a case or complaint reference number.
  • Record the date, time, and instructions you receive.
2

Secure affected accounts

If you shared login details, payment credentials, or remote access, treat account security as urgent.

  • Change compromised passwords using a trusted device.
  • Change reused passwords on other important accounts.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication where available.
  • Sign out unknown sessions and review account recovery settings.
  • Contact your mobile provider if you suspect a SIM swap or loss of phone-number control.
3

Preserve the evidence

Keep relevant records before deleting messages or closing accounts. Store copies securely and avoid altering originals.

  • Save receipts, statements, transaction IDs, and recipient details.
  • Preserve emails, messages, phone numbers, usernames, and website addresses.
  • Prepare a simple timeline of events.
  • Keep any case numbers and replies from providers or authorities.

4

Report the incident through appropriate channels

Report to the relevant financial provider, online platform, and appropriate authority in your country. A report does not guarantee reimbursement, but it may help document the incident and support an investigation.

  • Use official reporting websites and verified contact details.
  • Give accurate information and distinguish facts from suspicions.
  • Ask how to submit supporting evidence safely.
  • Keep a copy of your report and its reference number.

5

Monitor your accounts and follow up

Review statements, notifications, login activity, and credit or identity alerts where available. Follow up with your provider using your case reference.

  • Check for additional transactions or account changes you do not recognize.
  • Keep a record of each follow-up and response.
  • Ask about complaint escalation or dispute deadlines.
  • Respond promptly to legitimate requests for information, using secure channels.

Recovery steps by payment method

Different payment types have different rules. Contact the provider promptly and ask about the specific options that apply to your case.

Payment type What to ask the provider Important consideration
Bank transfer Can the transfer be recalled or traced? Can the receiving institution be notified? Is a formal fraud claim required? Transfers may be difficult to reverse once processed. A recall request is not a guarantee of recovery.
Debit or credit card Can the card be blocked or replaced? Can the transaction be disputed? What evidence and deadlines apply? The process depends on the transaction, card terms, local rules, and whether the payment was authorized or unauthorized.
Mobile money Can the transaction be flagged, traced, or escalated? What complaint process and reference number should you use? Act quickly and use the provider's verified customer-support channels.
Payment app or online wallet Can the payment be disputed? Can the recipient or account be reported? Can unauthorized access be stopped? Peer-to-peer payments and payments outside a platform's protected checkout may have limited protections.
Cryptocurrency Can the exchange secure your account, preserve records, or flag relevant wallet addresses? What incident-reporting process exists? Blockchain transfers are generally irreversible. Be cautious of anyone claiming they can guarantee a reversal.
Gift cards or vouchers Can the issuer check the balance, flag the card, or document redemption details? What records are needed? Keep the card, receipt, and redemption details private. Do not share unused codes with strangers.
Cash or cash courier If a legitimate transfer or delivery service was involved, can the transaction be stopped or reported? Recovery may be difficult. Preserve receipts, messages, and delivery information and report promptly.

What if you shared personal information?

You do not need to have lost money for a scam to create a security risk. Take steps based on the information you disclosed.

Password or login details

Change the affected password from a trusted device and review other accounts where you reused it.

  • Enable multi-factor authentication.
  • Review recovery email addresses and phone numbers.
  • Sign out sessions you do not recognize.

Bank or card information

Contact the financial provider using verified contact details and explain what was disclosed.

  • Ask whether the card or account should be blocked or replaced.
  • Monitor transactions and account alerts.
  • Never share your PIN or one-time authentication codes.

Identity documents

Seek guidance from the relevant identity-document issuer or local authority about possible misuse and protective measures.

  • Keep a record of what was shared and with whom.
  • Watch for unexpected applications or account activity.
  • Follow the official process for reporting identity misuse.

Remote access to a device

If someone remotely controlled your device, disconnect the session and seek trusted technical help if needed.

  • Remove remote-access software you do not trust.
  • Run reputable security checks and update your device.
  • Change important passwords from a different trusted device.

Recognize recovery scams

People who have already lost money may be targeted again by someone claiming to be able to retrieve it.

Warning signs to take seriously

  • Someone guarantees that your money will be recovered or promises a specific result without reviewing the facts.
  • You are asked to pay an upfront fee, tax, deposit, activation charge, or release fee to unlock supposed recovered funds.
  • A person claims to represent a bank, government agency, police unit, lawyer, or recovery firm but cannot be independently verified.
  • You are pressured to act immediately, keep the arrangement secret, or move the conversation to an unverified messaging account.
  • Someone asks for your password, private key, seed phrase, PIN, one-time code, or remote access to your device.
  • You are asked to send cryptocurrency or another payment to prove ownership or receive a supposed refund.

Verify any service independently. Do not rely on testimonials, screenshots, social-media followers, or documents supplied only by the person contacting you.

Frequently asked questions

Can I definitely get my money back?

No. Recovery depends on factors such as the payment method, timing, provider rules, the recipient's account, and applicable law. Some payments may be disputed or recalled, but others cannot be reversed. Contact the provider promptly and ask about your actual options.

Should I pay a recovery agent upfront?

Be extremely cautious. Upfront fees combined with guaranteed recovery claims are a common warning sign. Independently verify any organization and do not send additional money to an unverified person claiming to recover your loss.

Should I delete messages from the scammer?

Preserve relevant messages and records when possible. You can block or report the account after preserving evidence. If your device or account is at risk, prioritize safety and follow appropriate security guidance.

What if the payment happened several weeks or months ago?

Contact the provider anyway. Some options may have deadlines, so ask what remains available. Organize your records and report through appropriate channels even if you are unsure whether recovery is possible.

Does filing a police or cybercrime report guarantee a refund?

No. A report documents the incident and may support an investigation, but it does not by itself guarantee that funds will be returned. You may also need to contact the payment provider directly.

What should I do if I feel embarrassed or overwhelmed?

Scams are designed to manipulate people, and being targeted does not mean you deserved what happened. Consider asking a trusted person to help organize records or make calls. Focus first on preventing further loss and securing your accounts.

Take your next step

Contact the relevant payment provider, preserve your records, secure affected accounts, and report the incident through appropriate channels. There is no guaranteed recovery method, and you should never have to hand over passwords or private keys to seek help.
