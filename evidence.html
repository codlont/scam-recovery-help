Evidence Center

Organize the evidence. Protect the original records.

A clear record of what happened can help you explain a suspected scam to your bank, payment provider, platform, or law-enforcement agency. Use this checklist to prepare your information carefully.

Act quickly if money or account access is at risk

Contact your bank, card issuer, mobile-money provider, or payment service using its official contact details. Ask whether a payment can be stopped, recalled, disputed, or investigated. If an account is compromised, secure it immediately. Do not delay urgent action while collecting every item on this checklist.

Protect your privacy

Evidence can contain information that criminals could misuse. Share it only through appropriate, trusted channels.

  • Never publish passwords, PINs, recovery codes, or one-time codes.
  • Do not upload full identity documents to unknown websites.
  • Keep an unaltered original before making a redacted copy.
  • Check recipients and website addresses before sharing files.

Suggested folder structure

Create a private folder on a device or storage service you control.

  • 01 — Timeline
  • 02 — Messages
  • 03 — Payments
  • 04 — Screenshots
  • 05 — Reports and references

Use clear filenames such as 2026-10-29-payment-receipt. Avoid including sensitive personal details in filenames.

Keep your account safe

If you shared a password, reused a compromised password, or gave someone remote access to your device, treat that as a separate security issue and take steps to secure your accounts.

What useful evidence looks like

Clear, organized records help others understand the sequence of events without having to guess what each file means.

01

Clear context

Keep enough surrounding information to explain who communicated, what was said, and when it happened.

02

Accurate dates and amounts

Record the original currency, amount, transaction date, reference number, and payment method whenever available.

03

Originals and copies

Preserve original files. If you annotate or redact a copy for sharing, label it clearly and retain the original securely.

Important evidence safety rules

Gathering evidence should not put you at greater risk or interfere with a legitimate investigation.

  • Do not threaten, confront, or attempt to investigate a suspected scammer in person.
  • Do not hack accounts, bypass security controls, or access systems you are not authorized to use to obtain evidence.
  • Do not delete potentially relevant messages or files simply because they are upsetting. Secure your accounts and preserve what you can.
  • Do not send more money to someone who promises to recover your funds, unlock a wallet, or release a refund in exchange for an upfront fee.
  • Avoid sharing your complete evidence folder with strangers or unverified recovery services. A bank or authority may provide instructions about what to submit.
  • Follow local law and any instructions from your bank, lawyer, or investigating authority about evidence preservation and disclosure.

Where to take your records

Start with the organization that can act on the specific problem. Use independently verified contact details and official websites.

Ready to make a report?

Use your timeline and organized records to explain the incident. Report promptly, keep copies of any submission, and save all reference numbers. Recovery is not guaranteed, but prompt action may help protect accounts and preserve available options.
