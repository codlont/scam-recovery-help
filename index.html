Protect yourself

If you've just been scammed

Acting promptly may help protect your accounts and clarify what options are available.

What to do first

  • Stop communicating with anyone pressuring you to send more money.
  • Contact your bank or payment provider using its official website or app.
  • Ask whether a transaction can be stopped, disputed, or reviewed.
  • Change compromised passwords and secure affected accounts.
  • Save relevant messages, receipts, dates, and transaction references.
  • Consider making a report to the appropriate official authority.
Read the Recovery Guide →

What not to do

  • Do not send additional payments to unlock an alleged refund.
  • Do not share passwords, one-time codes, or wallet recovery phrases.
  • Do not install remote-access software at a stranger's request.
  • Do not trust guarantees that lost money will definitely be recovered.
  • Do not delete useful evidence before preserving a copy.
  • Verify contact details independently before calling or clicking.
Learn about scam warning signs →
Important: Recovery is never guaranteed. The options available depend on the payment method, timing, circumstances, and the organizations involved. Contact your financial provider promptly for advice specific to you.
