Security Center

Protect your accounts, devices, and personal information

Practical steps to secure your digital life, respond to suspected account compromise, recognize phishing attempts, and reduce the risk of further harm.

Start with your most important accounts. If you suspect someone has access to your email, banking, payment, or mobile accounts, use a trusted device and the provider's official website or app to secure them. If money is being taken or unauthorized transactions are occurring, contact the relevant bank or provider immediately.

Your first security steps

If you have clicked a suspicious link, shared a password, or noticed unfamiliar activity, work through these steps in order where possible.

1

Secure your email account

Your email account can be used to reset passwords for many other services. Secure it before moving on to less critical accounts.

  • Change the password from a trusted device.
  • Sign out unfamiliar sessions and devices.
  • Review recovery email addresses and phone numbers.
  • Check forwarding rules, filters, and connected apps for changes you did not make.
  • Enable multifactor authentication.
2

Protect financial accounts

Contact your bank, card issuer, mobile-money provider, or payment service through a verified channel if you suspect unauthorized access.

  • Report suspicious transactions immediately.
  • Ask whether your card, account, or payment credentials should be blocked or replaced.
  • Review recent transactions and payment recipients.
  • Change compromised credentials and secure linked email accounts.
  • Keep case numbers and transaction references.
3

Change exposed passwords

If you reused a password on several sites, change it everywhere it was used. Start with email, financial services, cloud storage, and social media.

  • Create a unique password for every account.
  • Use a reputable password manager if suitable for you.
  • Never share passwords or one-time verification codes with callers or messages.
  • Do not reuse an old password that may have been exposed.
4

Check your devices

If you suspect malicious software or remote access, avoid entering sensitive passwords on the affected device until it has been assessed.

  • Update your operating system, browser, and applications.
  • Remove unfamiliar apps or browser extensions after checking what they are.
  • Run a reputable security scan where appropriate.
  • Review remote-access software and remove access you did not authorize.
  • Seek qualified technical help if the device still appears compromised.

Password and sign-in protection

Use strong, unique passwords

  • Use long passwords or passphrases that are difficult to guess.
  • Use a different password for each important service.
  • Consider a reputable password manager to create and store passwords.
  • Never send passwords in chat messages or ordinary email.

Turn on multifactor authentication

Multifactor authentication (MFA) adds another step to sign-in. An authenticator app, passkey, or security key may offer stronger protection than text-message codes, depending on the service and your circumstances.

Keep recovery codes in a secure place. Never give an authentication code to someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

Review account sessions

Many services let you see devices, recent sign-ins, active sessions, and connected applications. Sign out unfamiliar sessions, revoke suspicious app access, and change your password if compromise is suspected.

Protect account recovery

Keep recovery email addresses and phone numbers current and secure. Check that nobody has changed your recovery options without permission. Store backup codes separately from the account they protect.

Recognize phishing and impersonation

Scammers may pretend to be banks, delivery companies, employers, technical support, government agencies, friends, or recovery specialists.

Warning signs

  • Unexpected messages asking you to click a link or open an attachment.
  • Urgent threats that your account will be closed unless you act immediately.
  • Requests for passwords, PINs, one-time codes, or remote access.
  • Payment instructions that suddenly change or use an unfamiliar recipient.
  • Web addresses that imitate a familiar organization but contain spelling changes or unusual domains.
  • Offers, prizes, refunds, or recovery guarantees that seem too good to be true.

How to respond safely

  • Do not click links or open unexpected attachments.
  • Contact the organization using a number or website you independently verify.
  • Do not rely on caller ID, profile photos, or a displayed sender name alone.
  • Report suspicious messages using the service's official reporting tools.
  • If you already clicked or entered information, secure the affected account promptly.
  • If you installed an app at someone's request, assess the device and revoke unauthorized access.

Check a website before signing in

Carefully inspect the domain name and open important services using a saved bookmark or by typing the known address yourself. A padlock or HTTPS connection encrypts a connection but does not prove that a website is legitimate.

Protect your phone and SIM

  • Use a screen lock and keep your phone's software updated.
  • Install apps only from trusted sources and review requested permissions.
  • Set a SIM PIN if appropriate, and ask your mobile provider what protections are available against unauthorized SIM replacement or number transfer.
  • If your phone unexpectedly loses mobile service, contact your mobile provider through a verified channel. Unexpected service loss can have several causes, but it may warrant checking for a SIM-swap incident.
  • Secure the email and financial accounts linked to your phone number.
  • Back up important files securely and understand how to recover your device if it is lost.
  • Avoid sensitive transactions on devices you believe are compromised.

Beware of recovery scams

Do not let a second scam make the situation worse

After a loss, you may be contacted by people claiming they can recover your money, trace a cryptocurrency transaction, hack a scammer, or release frozen funds. Some may use fake testimonials, forged documents, or official-looking profiles to appear credible.

  • Be suspicious of anyone who guarantees recovery or claims to have special access to your money.
  • Do not pay unexpected advance fees, taxes, clearance charges, or verification fees to unlock supposed recovered funds.
  • Never share your wallet seed phrase, private key, password, PIN, or one-time authentication code.
  • Do not install remote-access software at the request of an unsolicited caller or online contact.
  • Verify any lawyer, investigator, or service independently and understand the costs before agreeing.
  • Keep records of new suspicious approaches and report them to the appropriate platform or authority.

Legitimate banks, payment providers, law enforcement agencies, and other organizations have different processes. Verify instructions directly with the organization involved.

Frequently asked questions

What should I do if I gave a scammer my password?

From a trusted device, change the exposed password immediately. Change it on every other service where you reused it. Sign out unfamiliar sessions, check recovery settings, and enable MFA. If the account is financial or connected to payments, contact the provider if unauthorized access may have occurred.

What if I shared a one-time code or authentication approval?

Contact the affected service promptly through its official channel. Secure the account, review recent activity, revoke suspicious sessions, and change exposed credentials. Tell your bank or payment provider immediately if financial access may be involved.

Should I delete every suspicious message?

Not necessarily. If the message may be evidence, preserve screenshots, sender details, dates, links, and relevant reference numbers in a secure location. Avoid opening suspicious links or attachments. You can report the message through the relevant service's official tools.

What if I suspect my computer or phone has remote-access software?

Stop using the device for sensitive logins and financial activity until you can assess it. From a separate trusted device, secure important accounts and contact your financial provider if needed. Seek qualified technical assistance if you cannot confidently remove unauthorized access.

Can this website secure my accounts for me?

No. Scam Recovery Help provides general educational information. It does not access, inspect, or change your accounts, and this checklist does not send information to the website owner. Use the official service responsible for each account to make security changes.

Can I guarantee that I will recover my money?

No. Recovery depends on the circumstances, payment method, provider procedures, timing, and applicable law. Contact the relevant provider promptly and report the incident to an appropriate authority. Be cautious of anyone who promises a guaranteed outcome.
